What the yoke would be told

What is whose. In the aircraft X-Plane flies the airplane and reports the tail's angle and stall flags; the plugin reads them and computes the elevator's hinge moment and the yoke's force (decision 17). Here the aircraft panel is a stand-in for X-Plane: a longitudinal (pitch-plane) simulation flown from the yoke, so its wing stalls from angle of attack at any load factor, accelerated stalls included. The plugin is elevator.html's model block, copied unchanged (elevator_vectors.mjs checks it): it is given the tail's angle the stand-in would report (the wing's angle, plus the tail's setting, less the downwash, plus the pitch rate's share: no deflection in it, as TailAir assumes), the air over the tail, the load factor and the trim, exactly as elevator.html's state() builds them, and from those it computes the stalled share, the rest and what the drive is told (solve, 222 units per lb). The one thing judged here is its own stall: the tail's angle plus τ·δ past 13° over a 4° spread, which is the plugin's rule, not X-Plane's.

The stand-in's figures. Everything Flight has is taken from it: weight 2550 lb, area 174 ft², the wing's lift slope 4.55 per radian and zero-lift angle −2.1°, the stall at 17.3° with the lift blending into a flat plate's over 4°, the downwash 1.085·2CL/(π·AR), the tail's setting −2.5°, the tail arm 4.5 m, the thrust, and the air over the tail (Elevator.tailQ). What Flight lacks, each a guess at a C172's size: pitch inertia 1825 kg·m² (1346 slug·ft², the commonly quoted 172 figure, not checked against a copy) guessed; mean chord 1.47 m derived from the area and span; horizontal tail 3.28 m² (35.3 ft², stabilizer and elevator) published, not checked, lift slope 3.7 per radian for its aspect ratio derived; the wing's pitching moment −0.05 (NACA 2412) with the centre of gravity and the fuselage together 8% of the chord behind the aerodynamic centre, so the airplane's Cmα is about −0.95 per radian (the tail's −1.3 less the wing's and body's +0.36) guessed; a stalled wing's centre of pressure moving to mid-chord, so its moment goes nose-down as it stalls guessed; drag 0.03 plus the induced share, and a flat plate's when stalled guessed; the tail's lift stops rising past the section's stall, the same 13° and 4° as the plugin's rule, blending into a flat plate's guessed: X-Plane's tail does something of the kind, the figures are unknown; the tail's lift turns the airplane but is left out of the lift sum, so the wing carries the load factor as Flight has it assumed. Pitch damping comes only from the tail's pitch-rate share (Cmq −14.2 by finite differences), which gives a short period of 6.0 rad/s with a damping ratio of 0.58 at 110 kt derived; Roskam's C172 derivatives give 5.65 rad/s and 0.71. The altitude starts at 1000 ft and the air density stays sea level's assumed. A released yoke is the Brunner's: a 3 kg mass at the grip on the drive's line, refreshed every 10 ms, damped 0.6 of critical with 1 lb of friction guessed. Each run starts trimmed: the elevator at the angle that holds the speed level, the throttle at what holds it, and the trim tab set so the plugin's force there is zero (or as near as the tab reaches). Scripted manoeuvres move the yoke as a hand would; dragging takes over from them.

How to read it. The wing's stalled share is X-Plane's job: it comes from the wing's angle alone, at whatever speed and load factor the pull reaches, so a hard pull at 90 kt stalls it at 2 g and more. The elevator's own share is the plugin's rule and nothing else. The two force lines are solve with stall_deg 13 and 0 on the same state, both given X-Plane's tail stall flags as the plugin is (tail_stalled_xp: here the stand-in's section stall at the tail's angle alone), so their gap is what the rule adds beyond those flags; the drive's ceiling (drive_margin_units 4000 at pitch_force_units_per_lb 222: 18 lb at the far stop) clips both where the model asks more. Nothing here is X-Plane: what its tail panels report in a yank is step 0 of the plan.

What the runs show, and how sure that is. A full-aft snatch in 0.1 s at 110 kt takes the elevator's own angle to −12.1°, 0.9° short of the rule: the stand-in's pitch rate lifts the tail's angle within hundredths of a second. Only a snatch faster than a hand (20 ms) or a shove to the forward stop from a stall in 0.15 s or less passes it. Statically the rule fires at the aft stop whenever the reported tail angle is below +1° (−2° at cruise, so at cruise it is only the pitch rate that saves it), so a 1° difference in X-Plane's real cruise tail angle, from the tail's incidence or the downwash (step 0's measurement), could flip the 0.1 s case either way. And the drive's ceiling hides all of it: with the model asking 50 to 300 lb at a stop, both lines sit at 18 lb, and with X-Plane's flags carried by both the gap at the drive is zero throughout every run here (the 20 ms snatch peaks at a 56% own share, the 0.15 s shove at 74%, each for a few hundredths of a second). A frontier review integrating Roskam's C172 derivatives (Cmα −0.89, Cmq −12.4, Cmα̇ −5.2, Cmδe −1.28) agrees, and finds this stand-in errs toward firing.
