What the yoke would be told

Below the drive's 20 % floor:
Roll's drive has no constant-force register in the plugin's bounds, so it is told a spring and a centre only. Drive units from CLS2Sim's 0.001 N·m per unit and 0.006646° per count, inferred, not measured.

Figures and how sure they are. Aileron area 9.15 ft² each, from 18.30 ft² total (Jane's 1977–78, as recalled, to be checked against a copy); chord aft of the hinge 11 in (guessed from a 64 in wing chord and a Frise hinge set back). Hinge slope Chδ 0.30 per radian and float slope Chα 0.15 per radian: a Frise aileron's range in NACA Report 868 and TR 721, not a 172's (guessed). Travel 20° up, 15° down (Cessna 172 Service Manual, as commonly quoted, not yet checked against a copy); the linkage is a parabola through neutral and both stops, so the gearing is equal both ways at neutral and the differential grows away from it (a model of a differential bellcrank, not Cessna's drawing). Wheel travel 86° each way: the Brunner's own, from its soft limits and CLS2Sim's degrees per count; the 172's is a Service Manual figure (guessed). Grip radius 0.15 m (to be measured with a ruler). Weight moment 0 in·lb: the 172's ailerons carry balance weights (POH Section 7, as recalled); Cessna's balance limits are in SESRM 51-60-00 Table 1. Stalled share kept 40 %: no published post-stall hinge data; a guess for the owner to tune. Angles are clamped to ±30° and a panel with under 2.5 Pa of air says nothing (noise seen parked). Air from behind (an angle beyond ±90°) turns the deflection's moment over, so the aileron runs to a stop (decision 17, the owner, 2026-11-09; krev 1.0, guessed); the plugin does not do this yet. Prop wash over the ailerons 5 m/s at full power parked: they sit outside most of the slipstream (guessed). Inertia 0.10 kg·m² per aileron and 0.01 kg·m² for the wheel, damping ratio 0.4: rough. Roll rate and sideslip become per-wing angles here as p·y/V (y 4.3 m) and β times 1.73° of dihedral; in the aircraft X-Plane reports the angles and the plugin reads them.
