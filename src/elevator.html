What the yoke would be told

The drive takes a spring plus a constant force. The air on the deflection and the trim tab is the spring, centred on trim, at the strength the stalled share leaves (with air from behind it pushes away from trim); the weight and the float with the tail's angle are the constant force.

Figures and how sure they are. Weight moment: Cessna allows 0 to 43 in·lb trailing-edge heavy for both halves (Single Engine Structural Repair Manual 51-60-00, Table 1); no measured elevator was found, so 30 in·lb is a guess within it. Elevator area 14.5 ft² (Jane's 1977–78); chord aft of the hinge 17 in (derived). Air stiffness: hinge-moment slope 0.45 per radian, read from NASA TN D-7149 Fig. 39a, a similar Cessna-type high-wing elevator, not a 172's. Travel 28° up, 23° down. Yoke gearing 7.8° per inch, from a guessed 6.5 in of yoke travel. Inertia about the hinge 0.4 kg·m², below a flat-plate estimate (Roskam, NASA CR); prop wash over the tail and its damping are rough estimates. The trim tab's exact effect comes from X-Plane in the aircraft; here trim sets where the air holds the elevator.

The chain from the flight to the tail. In the aircraft X-Plane computes the tail's angle and its stall flags and the plugin reads them (tail panels 8 and 9, each weighted by its own air; decision 17); nothing here replaces that. The page computes the same chain so the yoke can be tried in a stall: each step a steady state (quasi-static), with no lag between the wing's angle and the downwash reaching the tail. Wing. 2550 lb, 174 ft², span 36 ft 1 in (C172S POH), so aspect ratio 7.48 published. NACA 2412 section: lift slope 6.0 per radian and zero lift at −2.1° (Abbott and von Doenhoff, Theory of Wing Sections) published; span efficiency 0.8 guessed; so the wing's slope is 4.55 per radian derived. The lift the airplane needs is the load factor times the weight, less the thrust's share along the lift (thrust 2.4 kN static, falling as 75% of 180 hp over the speed, guessed); the wing's angle follows on the straight line. The most lift, 1.54, is what the POH's flaps-up, power-off stall at 53 KCAS and 2550 lb needs derived, so the wing stalls at 17.3° to its chord derived; the POH's 48 KCAS is with full flap. Past that angle the stalled share grows to all of the wing over 4° and the lift blends into a flat plate's, 1.15·sin 2α (after Hoerner, Fluid-Dynamic Lift) guessed, so it falls. Past the stall the wing's angle is the one the held pull asks for, up to 24° guessed (the chain has no pitching moment to say where a full-up elevator holds a stalled wing), and the load factor actually reached is shown beside the one asked for: an accelerated stall shows as the angle passing 17.3° at a higher speed. Downwash behind the wing is 1.085 × 2·CL/(π·AR) guessed, chosen for dε/dα ≈ 0.42, within the 0.4–0.45 usual for a straight wing of this aspect ratio with the tail this far behind it (the textbook range, not a 172 measurement) published. It follows the lift, so when the wing stalls and its lift falls the downwash falls too and the tail's angle jumps. The tail's angle is the wing's, plus the tail's setting against the wing, less the downwash, plus the pitch rate times the tail arm over the airspeed. The setting, −2.5°, is chosen so cruise gives the tail about −2° (a download) guessed; the tail arm 4.5 m guessed from the 172's drawings. A steady pull pitches at g(n−1)/V derived; the pitch-rate slider adds a sudden pull's on top. Slower than 35 kt nothing holds a 172 in the air and that rate grows without limit, so flying, the chain answers at 35 kt or faster guessed; under it what the flight's air gives fades with that air's pressure into the aircraft with no airspeed: the tail's angle the wash's alone, as on its wheels, and the load factor 1. On its wheels the wing sits at 1.5° guessed and the downwash is that angle's. A wind from behind meets the prop's wash head on: their pressures subtract assumed, and when the wind wins the air over the tail comes from behind.

The tail's angle of attack and the stall. Float slope Chα 0.20 per radian, guessed: no C172 figure was found. It is set at a little under half of the hinge slope Chδ, the share plain flaps of this chord show in NACA's hinge-moment summaries (TR 721, Ames and Sears 1941, plain-flap and tab data; Report 868, as the aileron figures cite it), not read from a copy. The 172's elevator has horn balances at its tips, and a horn balance makes Chα smaller, even zero or of the other sign, so the knob goes below zero. NASA TN D-7149, the source of Chδ, may hold the same elevator's Chα; not checked. Positive Chα means the elevator floats with the air: a positive tail angle (air from below) lifts its trailing edge and moves the yoke's rest aft. The tail's own stall. The elevator adds to the tail's angle: the angle the tail section sees is its own plus τ times the deflection, τ 0.5, read from the usual flap-effectiveness chart (Raymer, Aircraft Design, from NACA plain-flap data) published for an elevator about 40% of the tail's chord, a share guessed from the drawings. A thin symmetric section stalls at about 12–14° (NACA 0009 and 0012, Abbott and von Doenhoff) published; the page uses 13°, the stalled share growing to all of the tail over 4° guessed. This is approximate and errs early: a deflected plain flap raises the section's most lift (Raymer gives up to about 0.9 for a plain flap), so in these terms the section stalls several degrees past 13°, and τ falls at large deflections; neither is modelled. A tail stall shown at full deflection, on the ground or in cruise, is the rule's upper bound on the risk; the knobs move it. Where the stall makes two balances, the elevator keeps to the one on its side of the unstable point between them, so a free yoke stays put as the air changes until its balance goes. In the aircraft the plugin uses X-Plane's tail stall flags if step 0 shows they follow the deflection; otherwise it computes this. Stall factor 40 % kept, guessed: no published hinge moments past a tail's stall (the NACA data stop at it); the ailerons' figure, for the owner to tune. It scales the air's moments, deflection, trim tab and float alike; the weight stays. Buffet is shown whenever any of the wing is stalled: its wake shakes the tail. It is a flag here; the drive is not told it. Gate 2.5 Pa and clamp ±30°: the model's own rules, the ailerons' (decision 4), derived from the noise seen parked. Step 0, assumed: X-Plane's tail panel angle is taken not to include the elevator's own deflection. If the parked sample shows it does, the plugin subtracts that share of elevator1_deg before the model sees the angle; the page is unchanged. Air from behind (an angle beyond ±90° with air over the gate) is modelled (decision 17, the owner's word of 2026-11-09): the deflection's moment turns over, krev 1.0 of it guessed, so the elevator runs away from trim to a stop, the one it is pushed toward from where it is, and the float takes the angle against the reversed stream. The weight starts it trailing edge down, so a straight tailwind leaves the yoke on the forward stop: the FAA's “dive away from the wind” for a tailwind on the ground (Airplane Flying Handbook, FAA-H-8083-3, taxiing). No spin: the chain has no yaw, no rotation and no blanketing of the tail by the fuselage, which are what make a spin, so a spin preset would show nothing true.
